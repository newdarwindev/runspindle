Candidate tasks and alternatives

  A promising study needs recurring work with a clear acceptance state, permitted access
  and enough value to cover its support burden.
    • Compare with an ordinary API or deterministic workﬂow when either can solve the
      task.
    • Preserve expert judgment where the judgment itself is the work.
    • Treat missing access as a boundary, not as a reason to automate around it.
    • Include exceptions, account changes and maintenance in the business case.


  Task-selection hypotheses only. No market size, adoption, pricing, customer, career or
  return-on-investment claim. A technically successful task can still cost more than its alternative.

  Use held-out task families, equivalent starting state, permissions and acceptance. Pin

Cost of accepted work


               C = M + T + H + V + K + R + F,                    U = C /A      when A > 0
   M, T              Model and tool costs
   H, V              Human task eﬀort and independent veriﬁcation
   K, R              Maintenance/support and recovery/repair
   F                 Fixed cost allocated to the period, including setup
   A                 Independently accepted, authorized completions in that period
  Each cost appears once, in the same currency and accounting period. Failures still cost
  money. At A = 0, unit cost is undeﬁned, including when C = 0.

  Pending eﬀects, unknown outcomes and correct stops are reported separately from A. Disclose labor
  rates, allocation and actual spend separately from elapsed time. Accounting identity, not a price,
  measurement or forecast.

Evidence needed for the decision

    • Separate the eﬀect of integration from the model, human assistance and task
      selection.
    • Exercise interruptions, recovery and independent acceptance on held-out services.
    • Compare quality, safety, latency and full cost after product upgrades.

  Current status records bounded synthetic and component evidence separately from
  blocked system acceptance. The integrated system is not implemented; live acceptance
  is not run.

  Editable source and authored tests support review; they do not prove runtime behavior. Historical
  examples are not new Runspindle results. Kapoor et al. (2024) motivates joint cost, quality and
  generalization checks. No comparative win is claimed.
